So the 28 is **not** a shape problem. Two concrete producers account for it:

1. **The `volatile` blocks the target's prologue hoist.** Target issues
   `lbu $v0,0x0($v1)` at `80061268` — *before* `sw $a1,%gp_rel(D_800A3468)` (`80061274`) and
   `sw $s0,%lo(D_800F1178)` (`8006127C`). A `volatile` load may not be scheduled across stores, so
   with `extern volatile u8 D_800F1159` in place cc1's sched1 **cannot** produce that order; the
   `lui/addiu $v1` pair and the `lbu` are pinned below the two stores and every subsequent slot
   shifts. Twin `func_8006133C` shows the same "constants first, `sw $ra` late, stores last" prologue
   emerging naturally with no volatile. Slug family: `legitimate-volatile-interrupt-touched` (the
   gate this decl has to pass, and probably fails).
2. **The pins distort the tail.** The sandbox strips `register … asm("$N")`, so `t` and `mask`
   become ordinary locals and RA re-picks; the six-instruction tail plus the `0xFF0060` interleave
   (`lui $v1` between `lw $v0,0x4($s0)` and its store, `80061308`) is scheduling-sensitive.
   Slug family: `register-alloc-pure-c`, `walking-pointer-serializes-parallel-loads`.

A secondary, smaller contributor: source order. Target materializes `&D_800F1159` into `$v1`
**first** (`8006125C/60`) and `&D_800F116C` into `$a1` second (`8006126C/70`), because the `lbu` is
the earliest consumer. Our C declares `s32 *v1 = (s32 *)&D_800F116C;` at `:3646` and touches it at
`:3649`, ahead of any `D_800F1159` use — the reverse order. Once the volatile is gone this may
resolve by itself via the hoist; if not, it is a one-line declaration/statement reordering
(`prologue-fix-redundant-reorder` documents that cc1 pairs each save with its first def **in source
statement order**).
