## 2. The refused-then-sanctioned spelling

Two distinct spellings were refused. **They are not the same construct, and F6 covers only
one of them.** This is the single most important fact in this brief.

### (a) `v1 += 2; v1 -= 1;` — the 16:14 form — **NOT covered by F6**

Banked at `memory/grind/func_80044098/rejected/judge-fail-0727-1613.c:890-895`, verbatim:

```c
            do {
                *v1 -= (s32)a6;
                v1 += 2;
                v1 -= 1;
                a4--;

```

The 16:14 ruling described it as "net +1 spelled as +1,+1,-1 … the operands 2 and -1 are a
fabricated cancellation-pair decomposition of the constant 1" (`decisions.md:1783`). It is a
**fabricated decomposition of the real operation**, not a cancellation pair: no statement
cancels, the net effect is the program's own `v1++`. F6's text sanctions "an adjacent
same-variable increment/decrement pair (`i++; i--;`)" — a pair whose net effect is *nothing*.
The `+= 2 / -= 1` spelling does not fit that description, and the still-standing
state.json judge_constraints[0] bans it by name:

> "The fabricated-constant cancellation/re-set-chain spelling (v1 += 2; v1 -= 1; and any
> same-register re-set chain whose constants are an invented decomposition of the semantic
> op) is UNSANCTIONED -- do not respell it"

**Do not use this variant.**
