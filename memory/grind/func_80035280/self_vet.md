## T1 semantic purpose
- `f`: no. `src = &D_80106A73 - 3;` is behaviourally identical. Flagged; family claimed below.
- `base`: it is the only handle the function has on the clock-record array, and the byte/stride arithmetic (`base[i*8]`, `*(s32 *)(base + i*8 + 4)`) requires a `u8 *` view of a symbol declared `extern s32`. Its *placement* above the loop is nevertheless codegen-motivated, so it is flagged and a family is claimed below rather than argued away.
- `flags0/flags1/flags2`: no. One re-used accumulator is behaviourally identical. Flagged; family claimed below.
- `mn/sc/hs/t`: no. Storing each expression directly is behaviourally identical. Flagged; family claimed below.
- `i = 0;`: YES. It is the loop's required initialisation — without it `i` is indeterminate and the function is wrong. Only its position in the statement stream is chosen; the statement itself is semantically load-bearing. Not a dead store, not a coercion, no family needed.
- `((u8 *)p + i)[0x17]`: YES. It computes the address that is stored to. Ordinary C.

## T2 human-programmer
- `mn/sc/hs/t`: a programmer formatting a minutes/seconds/hundredths clock naturally declares `u8 mn, sc, hs;` and a raw `s32 t` for the record byte. Nothing in the loop body reads as "why is this here?".
- `flags0/1/2`: three named results for three separately-published flag merges reads naturally; a reader sees three successive states of `p[8]`.
- `f`: a reader could ask why the base pointer is named before being offset. This is the one construct that does not pass T2 unaided, which is why it carries a `/* FAKE */` and a family claim.
- `base`, `i = 0;`, the address spelling: yes, all idiomatic.
